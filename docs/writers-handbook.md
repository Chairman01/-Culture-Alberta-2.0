# Culture Alberta — Writer's Handbook

The reference for VAs and writers. Read once before your first article, then use
it as a reference. If anything here is unclear or seems wrong once you're actually
doing the work, ask at the Sunday meeting or any time. Questions are always
welcome and this document is meant to change.

## 1. The site

https://www.culturealberta.com/

Culture Alberta covers Alberta — Edmonton, Calgary, Lethbridge, Medicine Hat,
Grande Prairie, Red Deer and Fort McMurray are the core cities. But all news
across Alberta and Canada can be covered.

We cover local news, events, business, and community stories. The tone is direct
and readable — we tell people what happened, not what to think about it.

## 2. What to write

Aim for around 5 articles a day, but this is flexible. Some days breaking news
carries the count; other days you'll write more evergreen. What matters is the
mix, not hitting the number exactly.

Three types of stories:

**Evergreen / money stories** — the most valuable. Government payments and
benefits (AISH, ADAP), rebates, taxes, stat holidays, "what's open on
[holiday]," deadlines, back-to-school planning. People search for these
constantly and the article keeps earning for years. Try to publish at least one
of these a day. If you're ever unsure what to write, write one of these.
Example: `/articles/alberta-energy-rebate-2026-who-qualifies-how-to-apply-and-when-you-get-paid`

**Local interest.** Business openings, new restaurants and stores, job roundups,
event guides, "coming to Edmonton/Calgary." Solid, steady performers.
Example: `/articles/edmontons-new-costco-is-under-construction-at-manning-town-centre-opening-timeline-the-50-street-que`

**Breaking news.** Fires, crashes, weather, crime, police incidents. These get a
lot of traffic fast and then stop. Worth writing, but they don't build anything
long-term, so don't let them crowd out the evergreen work.
Example: `/articles/severe-storm-hits-calgary-airdrie-and-crossfield-2100-without-power-quarter-sized-hail-and-damaging`

A useful habit on breaking stories: include a link to a related evergreen piece
or one of our tools. It gives readers somewhere to go next. Try to add videos and
photos for each article. Pay attention to weather alerts and see what is going on.

## 3. Finding stories

### Official sources — check daily

Everything we publish traces back to one of these:

- **Police:** Edmonton Police Service newsroom, Calgary Police Service newsroom,
  RCMP Alberta newsroom — https://x.com/edmontonpolice, https://x.com/CalgaryPolice
- **Government:** https://www.alberta.ca/news, Alberta Emergency Alert,
  Environment and Climate Change Canada
- **Municipal:** https://newsroom.calgary.ca/city-news-releases/,
  https://www.edmonton.ca/city_government/news-media, council agendas, transit
  and road advisories
- **Organizations:** the official site of whoever the story is about — the
  company, venue, festival, school board

### Other outlets — for spotting stories

Watch City News, Daily Hive Alberta, Narcity, and MTL Blog for the kinds of
stories that do well in this space. They're good for seeing what's getting
traction and what angles work.

Use them to find stories, then go get the official source yourself. If they have
a detail the official source doesn't, you can use it — just attribute it in the
text ("as reported by Daily Hive"). **Other outlets never go in the Sources block
at the bottom.**

### Social

Watch Urba, YEG Wave, YEG Scoop, plus official city and police accounts.

A social post is a tip, not a source. These accounts repost unverified things all
the time. Nothing from social gets published without official confirmation. If
you can't find it, the story doesn't run.

For now, log what you see rather than chasing it — a simple sheet with the date,
account, link, engagement, and a one-line note. Adam will pick from the log.
After a few weeks of watching what gets picked, you'll start to see the pattern
and can propose your own.

## 4. How to write an article

1. **Check we haven't already covered it.** Search existing articles using two or
   three distinctive words from the story, each on its own. If we've covered it,
   updating the existing article is usually better than writing a new one.
2. **Get the official source.** The actual police release, government notice, or
   company announcement. Read it yourself before writing.
3. **Draft it with Claude/ChatGPT.** Feed in the source along with the specific
   angle. Describe what the story actually is and make sure it does not read like
   AI slop.
4. **Verify everything.** This is the most important step. Open the source next
   to the draft and check with your own eyes:
   - Every number — dollar amounts, dates, times, ages
   - Every name — spelled exactly as in the release
   - Every claim

   If a fact appears in the draft that isn't in the source, take it out. AI adds
   plausible-sounding details that are wrong — a fine amount that's close, a date
   off by a week. This is the most common serious error and it's completely
   preventable here.
5. **Edit for voice.** Read it back in your head. Cut anything that sounds like a
   press release or like AI — "delve," "tapestry," "it's important to note,"
   "moreover," and any sentence that opens by restating the headline. Short
   paragraphs, concrete details early.

## 5. House style

### Headlines

- Put the exact phrase people would search in the first few words
- City or region early — Alberta, Edmonton, Calgary
- A specific number or detail as the hook
- Include the year (2026) on evergreen pieces
- One clean claim. Don't stack keywords with commas.

Give 3–4 title options with each article and note which you think works best for
search versus sharing. Titles can be changed later if something isn't working, so
don't agonize.

### Excerpt

Between 300–450 characters. Tease, don't summarize — make someone click, don't
save them the click.

### Sources block

At the bottom of every article. Official sources only — police, government, court
documents, the organization's own site. Other news outlets get credited in the
text instead.

### Tags

Minimum 3 per article. Cover the city, the topic, and the specific subject
(business name, program name, event name). Tags power our related-article links,
so an untagged article is stranded.

### Images

- **Featured image: 1600 x 900 pixels.** This one is a hard requirement — it's
  what the site and social cards expect.
- **In-article images:** any reasonable size. They just need to be clear,
  relevant, and placed near the part of the story they illustrate.

Use ChatGPT to sharpen or upscale anything that comes in soft. Credit images
properly, and never use one you can't account for. Real photos beat stock,
especially on local stories.

### Video

Video meaningfully improves how much an article earns, so it's worth the effort.

When a story has usable video, build it in Canva using the Culture Alberta style
(Adam will walk you through this on a call and give you access). Name it after
the article and flag it in your handoff — Adam adds it to the article.

Ask "is there video for this?" on every story. It's high value for the time it
takes.

### Polls

Add a 3-option poll where it fits.

**Never** add a poll to any story involving a death, crime with victims, harm to
children, sexual assault, a missing person, or live severe weather. On those
stories, turn comments off too. If there's a grieving family attached, no poll
and no comments. When unsure, leave both off.

### Legal wording

Any story naming an accused person includes: "The allegation has not been tested
in court." Every time.

On stories touching suicide, domestic violence, addiction, or eating disorders,
include help resources with phone numbers checked against the organization's
official site — not from memory or from an old article.

## 6. Publishing

You write and submit in the admin panel. Articles go live once Adam approves them
— so nothing you write goes public before it's been read. Write freely; mistakes
get caught.

Every edit or rejection comes back with a one-line reason. Read them. The same
note twice means something in the process needs changing.

Over time, as things settle, more will publish directly without review.

## 7. Getting stories out

### Who posts where

- **Reddit** — writers (see below)
- **Threads, Bluesky, X, Facebook, Instagram** — Adam

Finding new ways to get traffic is genuinely part of the job, not something to
wait for. Bring ideas to the Sunday meeting.

### Reddit

Reddit sends us a large share of our readers, and it's also the easiest to lose.
Moderators are strict about self-promotion, and a ban can block our whole site —
not just one account.

Post freely BUT DON'T SPAM:

- https://www.reddit.com/r/AlbertaWorkLife/
- https://www.reddit.com/r/Calgaryalberta/
- https://www.reddit.com/r/Albertapolitics/
- https://www.reddit.com/r/onguardforthee/
- https://www.reddit.com/r/GrandePrairie/
- https://www.reddit.com/r/AlbertaNewspapers/
- https://www.reddit.com/r/AlbertaNow/

And more, but talk to Adam if you have other places.

**Don't post for now:** r/Edmonton, r/Calgary, and the other large city
subreddits. You can read and comment there — useful for finding stories — but no
links to our site yet. If you're convinced a story belongs there, raise it Sunday.

Always:

- Never post the same story to more than one subreddit
- Write a real post with substance, not a bare link
- Don't post more than a couple of times a day
- When unsure, don't post. A story that misses Reddit still earns from search.

### Our own audiences

~1,200 newsletter subscribers and ~150 members. These are ours and can't be taken
away by a platform. Ideas for growing either are always welcome.

## 8. Newsletters

You prepare them, Adam reviews and sends. You won't have send access.

| Edition | Cadence |
| --- | --- |
| Edmonton, Calgary | Daily, once there are enough stories |
| Lethbridge, Medicine Hat, Grande Prairie | Weekly |

Consistency matters more than perfection — a predictable email gets opened out of
habit.

Building one:

- Pull stories for that city since the last send
- Lead with the strongest story, not the newest
- One or two lines per story — enough to make someone click
- Include an evergreen or benefits piece further down; those keep getting clicked
- Specific subject lines. "Edmonton: what's changing with AISH payments in
  September" beats "Your Edmonton update"
- Check every link before submitting. A dead link goes to everyone at once. Same
  fact-checking standard as articles.

## 9. Sunday meeting

Weekly team call. Send a written recap **before** the call, not after — writing it
makes you look at the numbers yourself, which is most of what makes this job get
easier over time.

What to send:

- What shipped this week
- Your top 3 by traffic, and why you think they worked
- Your bottom 2, and why you think they didn't
- Where the traffic came from
- What you posted to Reddit and how it did
- Newsletters prepared
- Anything you spotted but didn't write, and why
- One thing you'd try differently

On the call: you report first, Adam gives the numbers, then we set the coming week
and change anything in this handbook that isn't working.

## 10. Quick reference

**Every article:** check for duplicates → get the official source → draft →
verify every fact against the source → edit for voice.

**Every article has:** 3+ tags, a 1600x900 featured image, a Sources block with
official sources only, 3–4 title options, an excerpt of 300–450 characters, and
an Instagram caption starting "Click the link in bio for more."

**Poll:** yes, unless the story involves death, crime victims, children, sexual
assault, a missing person, or severe weather — then poll and comments both off.

**Named accused:** "The allegation has not been tested in court."

**Video:** build it in Canva, name it after the article, flag it.

**Never:** publish a fact you haven't verified, treat a social post as a source,
or post to Reddit outside the rules above.
